Class 1 · Beginner — class description

Finance & Investment Management

AIM · {{ site.program }} · 2 weeks · McGill University or Online · Beginner-friendly · Ages 13–18

How does money really work? What moves the stock market? And how do real investors decide what to buy? In two weeks and eight lessons, you'll find out — from a CEO who teaches finance at McGill.

This class starts with the psychology of money and the power of compound interest, then goes straight to what matters most for your future: investing and the stock market. Taught by Ilyas Zakiat — CEO of BIASafe AI, former portfolio manager and McGill finance lecturer — it turns complex ideas into clear, practical skills you'll use for the rest of your life. No experience needed: just curiosity.

Week 1

Money, Markets & the Investor's Mind

  • How money really works — the time value of money, simple vs. compound interest
  • The stock market & the mind — what a share is, what moves prices, investor biases, risk and diversification
  • Goals, risk & financials — your goals, your risk tolerance, and the five numbers that value a company
  • Reading the statements — balance sheet, income statement, cash flow and the analyst's checklist
Week 2

Building Your Portfolio

  • Building a portfolio — passive index investing, dollar-cost averaging, the blind portfolio challenge
  • Value investing — buying great companies on sale, and telling a bargain from a value trap
  • Growth investing — backing the companies of tomorrow, without paying too much for the future
  • Momentum & diversification — the habit that protects every strategy

Capstone — you'll manage a million dollars. A simulated $1,000,000 portfolio on a virtual stock exchange with real market data: one strategy, defined and defended in front of the class — and a CEO.

You'll leave with the tools real investors use, your own portfolio strategy, the confidence to talk about markets like a pro — and a certificate of completion.

Questions parents ask

Good to know before you apply

Does my child need any finance background?

No. The Beginner class starts from zero — what money is, what a share is — and builds up to a full portfolio strategy. Curiosity is the only prerequisite. Students who already invest will still learn a professional method they have never seen at school.

What ages can join?

Students aged 13 to 18 — from Secondary 2 to the first session of CEGEP. Under Quebec's privacy law (Law 25), students 13 and under are registered by a parent or guardian.

Is the program in English or French?

Classes, materials and the student portal are in English. Our team is bilingual, so parents are welcome to write to us in French.

Where exactly does the campus class take place?

On the McGill University campus in downtown Montreal. The exact building and room are confirmed by email before the session starts. AIM is an independent program run by Ilyas Zakiat, who teaches finance at McGill; it is not a McGill University program.

What does my child need to bring?

A laptop (or a tablet with a keyboard), a notebook, and lunch for the campus class. All software we use — the virtual stock exchange, spreadsheets, our student portal — is free.

Is there a certificate?

Yes. Every student who completes the two weeks and presents a portfolio receives an online certificate of completion signed by the instructor — useful for CEGEP and university applications.

Is real money involved?

Never. The $1,000,000 portfolio is simulated on a virtual stock exchange with real market data. Students learn the method without any financial risk.

How do we register and pay?

Start with the form below — it only tells us you're interested. We reply within two business days with the next steps. Registration and secure online payment open before each session; the full details are sent to parents by email.
